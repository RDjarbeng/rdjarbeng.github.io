## Africa Mindset Reset Forum: A Live Log

Reporting from the Africa Mindset Reset Forum. This post will serve as my log of events as they happen.

Since yesterday, August 25th, I've been at the Africa Mindset Reset Forum, hosted by the African School of Governance. The event is taking place at the Kigali Convention Centre in Rwanda, East Africa, with over 22 countries represented.

![Africa reset forum stage featuring orange and white theme at the Kigali Convention Center Auditorium](/assets/images/1000525267.jpg "Africa reset forum stage at the Kigali Convention Center Auditorium")

## Day One Discussions — August 25

A series of questions came up in the discussions on the first day, including:

- Where did we come from, how did we get here, and where are we going as a continent?

## Day 1 Summary

Francis Gatare, President of the African School of Governance, opened the forum by framing it as a reset moment for the continent. He pointed to the numbers behind that: 80% of participants are young leaders, and the inaugural cohort brings together 238 people from 22 African countries.

The first big panel, on transformative governance, put President Kagame, former President Thabo Mbeki, former Prime Minister Hailemariam Desalegn, and Ghana's Vice President Naana Jane Opoku-Agyemang on stage together. Opoku-Agyemang didn't hold back on bureaucracy: she said leaders keep forming committees to review the last committee's report instead of actually implementing anything, and pushed for staying pragmatic and holding institutions accountable. Kagame and the others picked up a similar thread, arguing that a lot of what holds Africa back is self-inflicted inaction, not just outside pressure.

After that came a session on what's actually being built right now. Dr. Joy Kategeka from the African Development Bank, Dr. Adam Azogenu of ACRA, and Richard Niwagara from the Rwanda Cooperation Initiative walked through three initiatives worth watching: the Africa Visa Openness Index, which tracks how borders are opening up across the continent; an African credit rating agency, meant to get away from foreign agencies that tend to overprice African risk; and Rwanda's own digital public services, held up as a model other countries could scale.

Then things shifted to an intergenerational dialogue, with Wamkele Mene of the AfCFTA Secretariat sitting alongside a group of youth leaders to debate what people are calling "Meta-Africanism." The youth in the room weren't shy about it, either. Their point was basically: we're already here, we're ready to lead, so drop the policy barriers standing in the way of cross-border trade and digital connection.

Journalist Charles Onyango-Obbo and Salahedin Bakour of the Panafrican Youth Union closed out the day. Their message: none of this "reset" talk means anything unless it shows up as real changes in incentives and turns into actual economic gains for young Africans.

## About the Forum

For context on the event itself: the Africa Mindset Reset Forum is a two-day gathering (August 25–26, 2026) hosted by the African School of Governance ([ASG](https://asg.ac/africa-mindset-reset-forum/)), co-founded by Rwandan President Paul Kagame and former Ethiopian Prime Minister Hailemariam Desalegn. It's bringing together roughly 1,000–2,500 delegates, including heads of state, former presidents, and senior policymakers, to discuss governance, institutions, and execution across the continent ([allAfrica.com](https://allafrica.com/stories/202608260049.html), [The New Times](https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/37777/news/rwanda/1000-leaders-to-meet-in-kigali-for-africa-mindset-reset-forum/amp)).

### A Note on Lithium Processing

I received some news that had gone under my radar: Zimbabwe has stopped the export of raw lithium.

This is confirmed ([electrive.com](https://www.electrive.com/2026/02/26/zimbabwe-imposes-export-ban-on-raw-lithium-and-lithium-concentrate/), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/25/zimbabwe-imposes-ban-on-exports-of-all-raw-minerals-and-lithium-concentrate)). In February 2026, Zimbabwe's government imposed an immediate ban on the export of raw minerals and lithium concentrate, accelerating a phase-out that had originally been scheduled for January 2027 ([Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/zimbabwe-imposes-immediate-ban-on-exports-of-raw-minerals-lithium-concentrates/)). The country is Africa's largest lithium producer.

Following the steps of Indonesia's  mineral export restrictions traced back to its 2009 Mining Law, with the actual ban on raw nickel ore exports first implemented in January 2014, then fully enforced from January 2020 ([USITC](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/332/working_papers/ermm_indonesia_export_ban_of_nickel.pdf)). 2010 was simply the year Indonesia became China's top source of raw nickel ore, not when any ban took effect ([UNCTAD](https://unctad.org/system/files/non-official-document/suc2017d8_en.pdf)). Zimbabwe's move echoes Indonesia's broader resource-nationalist strategy of banning raw exports to force local processing, just on a different timeline than I first cited.
