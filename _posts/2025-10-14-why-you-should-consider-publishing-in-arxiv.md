I recently came across a video on XploreBio by Dr. Abhishek titled *Why You Should Publish Papers in arXiv Repositories*. It got me thinking about the benefits of preprint publishing, and I wanted to share the main takeaways.

One point the video stresses is how time-sensitive research publishing can be. Getting your work out quickly matters if you want credit as the first to discover something and want others in your field to build on it.

That is where arXiv comes in. arXiv lets you upload a preprint of your paper, an early draft that has not gone through peer review. You can publish across fields like physics, biology, computer science, and economics, and it is free for anyone to read or submit.

### Key benefits of publishing on arXiv

Because papers on arXiv skip peer review before going online, your work appears right away. That helps when multiple groups are working on the same problem. There are no fees to publish or read, which makes it easier to share findings with the wider scientific community. Readers can also give you feedback early, so you can improve the paper before submitting it to a journal.

Some journals, including Elsevier, now let you submit to both their journal and arXiv at the same time. You do not have to choose between fast visibility and formal peer-reviewed publication; you can do both.

If you are planning to publish a paper soon, the XploreBio video is worth a watch. They also plan to release a tutorial on submitting to arXiv, which should be helpful if it is your first time using the platform.

For more details, check out the original video: ["Why You Should Publish Papers in arXiv Repositories"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-IPOgf5tBZw).
