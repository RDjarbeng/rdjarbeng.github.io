Free satellite data only helps if you know how to read it. Turning images of a flood, a dry farm or a growing town into information takes training. [NASA's Applied Remote Sensing Training Program](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/arset), known as ARSET, teaches these skills. Its online trainings have no course fee.

[Remote sensing](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/learn/trainings/fundamentals-remote-sensing) is the science of measuring Earth from satellites and other sensors. A sensor is an instrument that measures light or other energy. ARSET says its trainings build skills for air quality, public health, agriculture, disasters, ecological conservation, water resource management, climate resilience and wildland fires. Its [training list](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/arset/learn) can be filtered by topic, training level, training type and language.

## What ARSET provides

ARSET provides training rather than data. Its trainings move from basic satellite science to practical work, such as making a flood map.

There are three formats. A [live webinar](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/arset/training-types-levels) runs as a series of two to four parts, each 60 to 90 minutes long. Self-paced courses take 60 to 180 minutes and run in ARSET's learning management system, an online platform for lessons. In-person workshops last two to four days, with eight hours a day, and are hosted with partner organisations. They end with a small-group case study and presentation.

ARSET defines [four levels](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/arset/training-types-levels). Fundamentals needs no prior knowledge and explains how satellites and sensors work. Introductory courses show what satellite data can tell you about a topic, such as floods. Intermediate courses help you choose a suitable satellite-based product. Advanced courses show how to use satellite data to make a flood map.

The [Fundamentals of Remote Sensing course](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/learn/trainings/fundamentals-remote-sensing) is a 2-hour self-paced course. It teaches the basic functions of satellites and sensors, and uses NASA Worldview, a web tool for viewing satellite images. ARSET's [frequently asked questions](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/arset/contact) say that code from its trainings is shared on its GitHub page, so you can reuse it.

## What a certificate means

Certificates have conditions. For live trainings, participants must attend all live sessions and complete the homework by its due date, although ARSET can excuse one missed session. For self-paced courses, participants must complete all modules, pass the final knowledge check and fill in a post-training survey. ARSET says a certificate shows participation, not professional accreditation. Reviewing a past training does not earn a certificate. Treat a certificate as proof that you took the course, not as a professional qualification.

## Examples from Africa

ARSET ran a five-day, in-person workshop in Cape Town, South Africa. It was held in collaboration with the ORNL DAAC data centre and the BioSCape campaign. The [2024 annual report](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-07/AnnualReport2024.pdf) describes the workshop. The first two days covered field spectroradiometers, which measure light from land and water targets, and gave a first look at hyperspectral imagery from BioSCape. Hyperspectral imagery records light in many narrow bands. Participants learned to use field spectral data to validate airborne imagery, which means checking aircraft images against measurements taken on the ground.

The same report quotes a student in Ghana who took a water quality training in 2024. The student planned to study how sediment from illegal mining affects Ghana's marine waters. This was a stated plan, not a completed study. It shows how a short training can prompt a local research plan, though the outcome is not yet known.

## How to start using ARSET

Open the [ARSET page on NASA Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/arset). Use the filters for topic, level, training type and language to find a course.

For a first course, open [Fundamentals of Remote Sensing](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/learn/trainings/fundamentals-remote-sensing). It is a 2-hour self-paced course. If you do not have an account for the ARSET learning management system, you will be asked to create a free one.

Online trainings are open to all at no cost, as the [FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/projects/arset/contact) states. You can review past slides and recordings at any time. In-person trainings are hosted with partner institutions, and ARSET cannot offer financial help to attendees, so plan travel costs yourself.

## Conclusion

Free satellite data only helps if you can read it, and ARSET teaches that for no course fee. Start with Fundamentals of Remote Sensing, then choose a course that matches the problem you need to solve.
