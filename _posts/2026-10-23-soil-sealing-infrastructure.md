Soil sealing-free infrastructure refers to construction, engineering, and urban planning methods that avoid covering the ground with impermeable materials (such as concrete, asphalt, or dense stone pavers), allowing the soil to remain open, breathable, and functional. [[1](https://www.fao.org/world-soil-day/key-messages/en), [2](https://www.cipra.org/media/files/groundbreaking_handbook-en-2-7-2025.pdf/@@download/file/GroundBreaking_handbook%20%28en%29%20-%202.7.2025.pdf)]

Core Concepts & Alternatives

- **Permeable Paving:** Using porous asphalt, pervious concrete, interlocking grass pavers, or gravel grids that let water and air pass through to the earth below.
- **Elevated Structures:** Building boardwalks, raised pathways, or pile-supported foundations rather than solid grade-level slabs to preserve root zones and natural drainage.
- **Brownfield & Infill Prioritization:** Directing new construction onto previously sealed lands (like old parking lots or abandoned industrial sites) instead of greenfields. [[1](https://resoilfoundation.org/en/environment/de-sealing-free-soil-best-practice/), [2](https://www.recare-hub.eu/soil-threats/sealing.html)]
- **De-sealing (Unsealing):** Ripping up redundant asphalt or concrete surfaces and restoring them with native vegetation, rain gardens, or porous soil. [[1](https://cordis.europa.eu/programme/id/HORIZON_HORIZON-NEB-2027-02-NEB-SOIL-01), [2](https://resoilfoundation.org/en/environment/de-sealing-free-soil-best-practice/)]

Key Environmental Benefits

- **Water Management:** Prevents surface runoff and flash floods by allowing rainwater to soak into the ground and recharge aquifers.
- **Urban Heat Island Mitigation:** Keeps surface temperatures lower compared to heat-absorbing black asphalt and concrete.
- **Biodiversity Protection:** Preserves habitats for earthworms, beneficial microbes, fungi, and plant roots.
- **Carbon Storage:** Keeps organic carbon locked inside the top layers of natural soil rather than releasing it or destroying the sequestration cycle.
