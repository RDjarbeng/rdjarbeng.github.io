Andrew Ng defines AI engineering not merely as a standalone job title, but as a critical, evolving skill set that benefits professionals across all software domains, including front-end, back-end, DevOps, and mobile development. While traditional software engineering focuses on building and maintaining deterministic systems, AI engineering specifically addresses the uncertainty inherent in AI-powered applications, where outcomes are not always predictable in advance.

Ng argues that the role of a developer is shifting from pure implementation to a more holistic process of deciding what to build. Even as coding agents handle increasing amounts of rote implementation, fundamental engineering knowledge remains essential for managing trade-offs like latency, cost, and reliability that AI might otherwise overlook.

To master this field, Ng's team at DeepLearning.AI developed a skills map centered on four foundational pillars:

Building and deploying AI applications: Learning to manage the inherent uncertainty of AI systems through systematic monitoring, error analysis, and evaluation loops.
Software engineering fundamentals: Maintaining a strong grasp of core principles, such as transaction management and rewrite ratios, to effectively steer and debug code written by AI.
Ability to use coding agents: Developing the proficiency to leverage, prompt, and manage AI coding agents to enhance productivity.
Shaping the build: Transitioning into a role where the engineer influences product and architectural decisions, moving beyond simple implementation to actively guide the development process based on product instinct and user feedback.
